\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis investigated integrating gradient projection
methods into chunked hypernetworks for continual learning.
We primarily diagnosed the architectural boundaries
and numerical pathologies governing this core integration.
Alongside these fundamental structural findings, we
also evaluated adjacent methodological optimizations regarding
parameter inertia and Fisher matrix accumulation.

\paragraph{Sub-RQ1 --- Partial Synergy and the Momentum Bottleneck.}
The synergy between hard gradient projection and
soft functional regularization is constructive but bounded.
While \texttt{iFOPNG} outperforms unconstrained \texttt{SGD}
in standard-capacity settings, standalone regularized
hypernetworks optimized via \texttt{Adam} remain
vastly superior.

This performance gap stems from an architectural
conflict, not projection geometry.
Under extreme compression ($d_h = 8$), Fisher-based
projection methods suffer a severe \emph{plasticity}
failure, exhausting the gradient budget and halting
new task acquisition.
Because projection modifies raw gradients before the
optimizer updates, it discards adaptive momentum.
In hypernetworks, this momentum acts as a crucial
structural regularizer; without it, the network commits
prematurely to poorly-conditioned gradient directions.

Ultimately, the loss of adaptive momentum is
the binding constraint.
Integrating gradient projection into hypernetworks
will require projection-compatible adaptive optimizers,
rather than post-hoc gradient modifications \citep{hu_hidden_2026}.

\paragraph{Sub-RQ2 — Normalization is a necessary condition.}
Without explicit local rescaling, projection methods are numerically
incompatible with chunked hypernetworks: matrix inversion in the projection
kernel collapses completely, degrading performance below plain SGD.
QR orthonormalization of the gradient basis combined with absolute-maximum
Fisher scaling resolves the pathology entirely.
This normalization scheme is a necessary engineering precondition for any
future combination of gradient projection with chunked meta-learners,
regardless of which projection algebra is employed.

\paragraph{Sub-RQ3 --- Parameter Inertia via Combined Fisher Metric.}
Replacing the task-specific Fisher metric with the combined inertial metric ($F_c$)
systematically improves retention as task difficulty and output-level interference
increase. On simple benchmarks, both methods are statistically indistinguishable.
On harder standalone benchmarks like Split-CIFAR10, \texttt{iFOPNG} provides
a clear, statistically significant advantage ($+13.8$~pp, $p < 0.05$). This
substantial raw advantage carries over into the hypernetwork settings, yielding
performance gaps of $+11.8$~pp and $+8.6$~pp. However, these specific hypernetwork
improvements do not reach statistical significance due to elevated inter-seed
variance. Ultimately, integrating parameter inertia yields a consistent retention
advantage that scales directly with benchmark complexity. % THIS CAN STILL BE IMPROVED.


\paragraph{Sub-RQ4 — MAX accumulation is the clear practical choice.} 
Addressing the trade-off between long-horizon retention and plasticity, the element-wise maximum (MAX) 
operator proved systematically superior to an exponential moving average(EMA). While the absolute performance
advantage is small ($\sim$$1.60$~pp) on a twenty-task sequence, it is statistically unambiguous across
all seeds. Crucially, the hypothesized plasticity cost associated with the monotonic growth
of the MAX operator did not materialize over the extended task sequence, likely because the
chosen normalization scheme minimizes sensitivity to absolute scale. Therefore, MAX emerges as the definitive practical
choice: it avoids plasticity degradation while remaining a simpler, hyperparameter-free, and marginally superior alternative.

\paragraph{Implications for the field.}
The results establish a clear empirical boundary for the current generation
of gradient projection methods: they can be made numerically stable in
chunked hypernetwork settings via local rescaling, and their Fisher geometry
can be improved via the combined Fisher metric $F_c$, but the momentum incompatibility
prevents them from reaching the performance frontier of adaptive
unconstrained optimizers in generative parameter spaces.
The normalization contribution is directly applicable to any method that
maintains a gradient subspace memory alongside a chunked weight generator.
The iFOPNG contribution extends naturally to any continual learning
setting where Fisher overlap accumulates across tasks.
Together, they narrow the gap between projection-based and
regularization-based approaches, and provide a principled foundation for
the next step: a projected adaptive optimizer that respects both the
geometric constraints of the projection and the curvature structure that
adaptive moment estimates capture.

\paragraph{Future directions.}
The most immediate extension is developing
projection-compatible adaptive optimizers that maintain
momentum across constrained updates \citep{hu_hidden_2026}.
Additionally, the heuristic QR normalization, FIM max scaling scheme
must be replaced with a principled reformulation
that integrates chunk-Jacobian curvature directly
into the projection algebra.
Finally, future work should systematically evaluate
these interactions on extended task sequences,
where soft regularization penalties may degrade,
and on larger target architectures,
where the documented condition number blowup
will require more robust mitigation.
